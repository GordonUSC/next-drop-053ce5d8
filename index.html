The meta right now

What changed in solar, and where the demand went. Checked November 17, 2026.

The short version. The 30 percent federal credit for homeowners ended on December 31, 2025, and home solar slowed across the country. Demand moved to batteries, utility programs that pay customers for their stored power, heat pumps, and commercial projects. That is the part of the business you already sell. You are not starting over. You are early.
Read the details and sources
The federal residential clean energy credit (Section 25D, the 30 percent homeowner credit for solar and batteries) is not allowed for any expenditures made after December 31, 2025, under Public Law 119-21. The IRS FAQ lists this termination date; the related 25C home improvement credit ended for property placed in service after the same date.Source: irs.gov, 2025-08-21
Reuters reported in February 2026 that US residential solar companies were preparing for a steep drop in business after the homeowner credit expired, with layoffs, restructurings and some company failures; Enphase said it would cut 160 jobs, about 6 percent of its workforce.Source: reuters.com, 2026-02-05
Washington's solar trade group says that after 25D ended, a large federal credit for home solar is available only through third-party-owned systems (leases and power purchase agreements), which pushes the market away from the cash and loan sales that built Washington residential solar. This is WASEIA's characterization; I did not verify the commercial credit deadlines against the statute, so treat the timing as unconfirmed.Source: waseia.org, undated page, read 2026-11-17
Washington net metering: utilities must offer retail-rate net metering until June 30, 2029 or until net-metered capacity reaches 4 percent of the utility's 1996 peak demand, whichever comes first (RCW 80.60.020). After that a utility may keep it or propose a different tariff. Commerce says several utilities have reached the threshold but only a few have moved away from retail rate.Source: commerce.wa.gov, undated page, read 2026-11-17
The Washington State Academy of Sciences delivered its legislatively directed study of the value of distributed solar and storage in October 2026. It notes Washington utilities reaching the 4 percent cap, a national residential battery attachment rate that rose from 9 percent in 2023 to 25 percent in 2024, and regional load forecasts now above 30 percent growth over the coming decade. Any successor to net metering would come later through the UTC, utility boards or the Legislature.Source: washacad.org, 2026-10-23
Secondary source, not checked against utility tariffs: a Washington battery installer's guide reports PSE at 132 percent of the 4 percent level per a January 2026 WSU Energy Program report, Pacific Power proposing net billing for applications after February 1, 2027 (pending at the UTC), Seattle City Light granting lifetime net metering to applications before May 1, 2027, the solar sales tax exemption (RCW 82.08.962) running through December 31, 2029 for systems of 100 kW or less, and no state battery rebate (HB 1871 and SB 5727 not passed).Source: pacwestbattery.com, 2026-11-01
Virtual power plants are now a live utility product in your territory: PSE Flex Batteries pays up to $1,000 per battery at enrollment plus up to $500 per year for participating in Flex events, and enrolls SolarEdge, Tesla, Enphase and FranklinWH batteries as part of what PSE calls its virtual power plant.Source: pse.com, undated page, read 2026-11-17
Storage keeps growing while rooftop solar shrinks: SEIA's site reports 14.3 GWh of new US energy storage installed in Q3 2026 and 48.3 GWh in the first nine months of 2026, passing 200 GWh installed to date. This is all segments, mostly utility scale, not residential alone.Source: seia.org, read 2026-11-17
CETA (2019) commits Washington to an electricity supply free of greenhouse gas emissions by 2045 with a greenhouse-gas-neutral standard in 2030; Commerce is running its four-year assessment now and took public comment through October 29, 2026. I found no sourced count of CETA-driven utility hiring. My own observation today: PSE's careers site shows 46 openings, including renewables development and solar major-projects engineering roles, and no customer energy program roles.Source: commerce.wa.gov, read 2026-11-17

Likely more than 90 days unless the training is already done.Storage is where the growth is. Caution: written for installers; NABCEP decides whether a sales and design role counts as decision-making.
